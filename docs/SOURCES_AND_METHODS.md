# Earth Analogs Explorer: sources and methods

Every dataset, reference and processing step in the project, followed by the "why" questions. Written 2026-11-15 from the data files, the pipeline scripts and the README. Where I could not confirm something from the files, it says so.

## Part 1. Sources

### Earth grid (1 degree, 360 x 180, docs/data/grid.json + *.bin)
| Feature | Source | How it becomes a 1-degree value |
|---|---|---|
| slope_p90, slope_median, relief, hypso | NOAA NCEI ETOPO 2022 Global Relief Model, ice surface, 60 arc-second. https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/etopo-global-relief-model | build_grid.py: inside each 1-degree cell, slope from the 60 arc-second (about 1853 m) pixels; p90 and median of those slopes; relief = highest minus lowest; hypso = hypsometric integral |
| ts_mean, ts_range | NASA MODIS Terra MOD11C3 v6.1 land surface temperature, day and night, monthly, 12 months of 2025 averaged (LP DAAC). https://doi.org/10.5067/MODIS/MOD11C3.061 | prep_lst.py: mean = (day + night) / 2, range = day minus night |
| ts_day | Derived from the MODIS values above: ts_mean + ts_range / 2 | build_grid.py --derive-only |
| t_mean, t_range | WorldClim 2.1 (1970-2000), BIO1 annual mean and BIO7 annual range, air temperature. https://www.worldclim.org/data/worldclim21.html | Shown, never scored (the Moon has no air) |
| albedo | NASA MODIS MCD43C3 v6.1 BRDF/Albedo, white-sky shortwave albedo (0.3-5.0 um), 0.05 degree, one 16-day retrieval per month of 2025 (files A2025015 to A2025349). https://doi.org/10.5067/MODIS/MCD43C3.061 | prep_albedo.py: months with a value averaged per 0.05 degree pixel, then the 400 pixels of each 1-degree cell averaged. Snow and ice are included on purpose |
| Place names | Natural Earth 1:10m Admin 0 and Admin 1, public domain. https://www.naturalearthdata.com/ | build_places.py: the province (else country) covering most of each cell |

### Curated, not measured
- Geology tags and the curated analog pairs (Shackleton to Devon Island, and so on) are the team's own judgment. No citation yet. Tags are weight 0, so they do not change any score.
- Site coordinates carried over from the original dataset are not yet checked against a gazetteer, except Tranquility (NSSDC). Their coord_src fields say so.

### Our own rules (not from a source)
- Feature scales (slope_p90 15, slope_median 10, relief 2000, hypso 0.5, ts_mean 120, ts_range 60, ts_day 120, albedo 0.3): starting guesses. Changing them changes rankings.
- Question-preset verdict cut-offs (80% close, 60-80% partial, below 60% none): our rule of thumb, labelled as such in the app.

## Part 2. Why and how

**Why does the Moon temperature map have patches missing?**
The Diviner product is a table of temperature by position and local time of day, built from 2009-2015 passes. We only take two local-time windows, 10:15-10:45 and 22:15-22:45, to match the MODIS overpass times on Earth. In some places the orbit never observed that window often enough, so the bin is empty. They show as scattered strips, about 8% of the Moon. We leave them empty. When less than half of a 111 km patch has data the whole point is left empty. We do not interpolate or fill from neighbours, because that would be an invented number. Near the poles local time says where the Sun is in the sky, not whether it is up, so polar values (Shackleton's small swing) need care.

**Why is there no global Mars temperature map?**
A decision we made. TES temperatures come from a per-region query (the TES Data Tool emails a file), so we have them for three sites only. Mars points you pick are compared on terrain, landform and albedo, and the app says why temperature is missing.

**Why is there no Moon albedo?**
The only file we had is not an albedo map. A missing value is better than a wrong one. Presets that use albedo score the Moon on the other features and say so.

**How was a Moon or Mars point measured?**
For each 1-degree point, a 111.2 km patch of 60 x 60 points spaced 1853 m apart is sampled bilinearly from the source map. Slope, relief and hypsometric integral are calculated on that patch. Spacing and patch size match one Earth ETOPO cell (60 arc-second pixels, 1 degree), so both sides are measured on the same ground size.

**Why 1853 m?**
It is 60 arc-seconds on Earth, the ETOPO pixel size. Slope depends on the baseline it is measured over, so all bodies use the same one.

**Caveat on that.** The Moon grid DEM (LDEM_16) is about 1.9 km per pixel, so there it is almost exactly the baseline. The Mars grid uses MOLA 463 m while the three Mars targets use the 200 m HRSC blend; both are averaged to 1853 m before slope, but the two Mars sources are not identical. Worth a note in the pitch.

**How is the score calculated?**
Per feature: 1 minus min(1, |gap| / scale). Features within a dimension share its weight. Dimensions (terrain, landform, thermal, day thermal, albedo) count equally. An Earth cell is scored only if it has a value in every dimension in use, so a coastal cell cannot reach 100% on temperature alone.

**What does "top 0.1% of Earth" mean?**
Share of scored land cells that score lower, rounded up. The tooltip gives the position ("cell k of N").

**Why do some presets show near 100% everywhere?**
Few features means little to disagree on. A preset with two features is easy to match. The Thermal preset on Tranquility gives 50% because no Earth cell matches both a 278 C swing and the mean.

**Why is the Earth temperature from MODIS land surface temperature and not air temperature?**
The Moon has no air. Surface (skin) temperature is the like-for-like quantity. WorldClim air temperature is shown but not scored.

**How reliable is the albedo?**
Earth white-sky albedo and Mars TES bolometric albedo are the closest pair available, not identical (band edges, TES afternoon data, TES values clipped to 0.06-0.32). The 1-degree Earth cell mixes everything in it, ice included, which is why Holuhraun drops for Jezero (the cell includes Vatnajokull) and Mauna Loa vs Olympus gets worse (dark basalt vs bright Mars dust). Backtest median rank changed from 21% to 16% when albedo was added.

**What does the backtest show?**
For each curated target-analog pair, where the analog's Earth cell ranks among all scored cells. It tests whether the score finds places scientists already call analogs. Pairs that rank low are chosen for reasons the grid does not measure (chemistry, ice, history).

**What is the "where Earth has no analog" map?**
build_gap_map.py: for every Moon or Mars point, the best Earth score anywhere, using the same rule as the website. Moon has terrain and terrain+temperature layers; Mars has terrain and terrain+albedo (the albedo layer is what a Mars click compares on). Adding albedo barely changes the Mars result: Earth still reaches 80% or more over about 99% of Mars, because what makes Mars hard to match (temperature, thin air) is not in the Mars grid. Moon cells are clustered toward Antarctica, a real result of the data.

**What can a high score not tell you?**
Earth cannot reproduce gravity, vacuum, thin Mars air or radiation. A high score means the measured surface matches, a lead to check, not a verdict.

**What is still weak?**
- Scales and thresholds are guesses.
- Curated pairs and tags have no citations.
- Coordinates for most named sites are unverified.
- 1-degree cells average away small features.
- Mars temperature covers 3 sites; Moon albedo is absent.
- Earth values are from 2025 (MODIS) and 1970-2000 (WorldClim); Moon from 2009-2015.
